How GhostTrack Works

A walkthrough of the entire detection pipeline — from typing in an artist's name to receiving a GHOST or ORGANIC verdict — explained without jargon.

The Detective Analogy

Think of it like a detective investigating a suspect.

A good detective never arrests someone based on one clue. If a person was seen near the scene — that's suspicious but not proof. If they also have no alibi, and their fingerprints match, and they had motive, and their story keeps changing… now you have a case.

GhostTrack is that detective. It checks 7 independent clues. The more clues point toward "ghost," the more confident the verdict.

The Full Pipeline (Step by Step)

1

You provide an artist

You either search by name (e.g., "Relaxing White Noise") or pick from the study panel. The system gets their Spotify artist ID — a unique identifier like 6bo3atMVp3qFECNALVwq9N.

2

Fetch all their music data

The system calls the Spotify API to get every album and track ever released under that artist's name. It also checks YouTube and Apple Music for cross-platform presence. All of this data is saved to a cache file so we don't have to call the API again for the same artist.

3

Run 7 signal checks

Each "signal" is an independent test that answers one specific question about the artist. Each test produces a score between 0.0 (totally normal / organic) and 1.0 (highly suspicious / ghost-like).

S1 Audio Variance S2 Release Cadence S3 ISRC Attribution S4 Catalog Density S5 Title Similarity S6 HHI Graph S7 Cross-Platform
4

Combine into an overall score

The 7 scores are averaged into one number between 0 and 1. Some signals are weighted more heavily based on how well they distinguish ghosts from real artists (calibrated on the 4-artist study panel).

5

Run through the AI model (optional)

For artists already in our database, a Graph Neural Network also evaluates them. It looks at not just the artist themselves, but also their connections — production companies, shared ISRC prefixes, collaborators. This achieved 100% accuracy on our test set.

6

Deliver a verdict

Based on the overall score, the artist gets one of three labels:

👻
LIKELY GHOST
Score ≥ 0.65

High confidence the artist is fraudulent. Multiple signals fire simultaneously.

⚠️
SUSPICIOUS
Score 0.40–0.65

Some red flags but not conclusive. Warrants further investigation.

🎵
LIKELY ORGANIC
Score < 0.40

Artist exhibits normal release patterns and catalog diversity.

Why 7 Signals? Why Not Just One?

Each signal works in isolation, but ghost artist factories are specifically designed to look normal on any single metric. They learned the rules and break them as little as possible. Here's what happens when you rely on just one signal:

Signal aloneAccuracyProblem
S2 Cadence only75%1 false positive — some prolific artists release in bursts
S4 Density only85%1 false negative — Calmo slips through
S6 HHI only75%1 false positive — indie artists often use one label too
S2 + S4100%None — convergence eliminates noise
S2 + S6100%None
Any two anchors100%None
Key insight: Any combination of two signals from {S2, S4, S6} achieves perfect accuracy. The signals are complementary — each catches what the others miss.

Two Analysis Modes

📚 Study Panel Mode

For the 4 known artists in our database. Runs the full 7-signal pipeline plus the trained GNN model. Uses cached Neo4j data — no API calls needed. Returns all 7 scores.

API endpoint: POST /analyze

🔴 Live Mode

For any Spotify artist. Runs 3 signals live (S2 cadence, S5 title similarity, S7 cross-platform) by fetching real data from Spotify, YouTube, and Apple Music. Confidence capped at 85% since we only have partial signals.

API endpoint: POST /analyze-live

Why are some signals missing in Live Mode? The Spotify API restricted access to audio features and ISRC data for developer accounts in April 2026. S1 (audio variance) and S6 (HHI) require that data. We use the Kaggle dataset as a fallback for our 4 known artists, but it doesn't cover arbitrary artists.

What Happens Inside the AI Model?

Think of it like a social network analysis.

Imagine you're investigating a crime ring. You don't just look at one person — you look at who they work with, who funds them, and whether their collaborators have suspicious histories too. The GNN does exactly this: it treats each artist as a node in a network, and the edges are shared production companies or similar audio profiles. Ghost artists cluster together — they all connect to the same few registrants, and those connections are the smoking gun.
