The 7 Detection Signals

Each signal is an independent test that asks one specific question about an artist. Scores run from 0.0 (completely normal) to 1.0 (maximally suspicious). Click any signal to learn how it works, what data it uses, and what the numbers mean.

S1

Audio Feature Variance

Do all this artist's songs sound identical? · Strong Signal
Think of it like a cookie factory vs. a home baker.
An industrial cookie factory produces thousands of identical cookies — same shape, same size, every time. A home baker makes different shapes, sizes, and flavors. Ghost artist factories are like cookie factories: they use the same AI settings to generate every track, so all the songs have nearly identical audio profiles.

What it measures

Spotify assigns every track 6 numeric features: danceability, energy, valence (happiness), acousticness, speechiness, and instrumentalness. For a real artist, these vary a lot across their catalog — their slow songs are very different from their dance tracks. For ghost artists, the values stay almost constant because it's the same AI generating everything.

We compute the total variance across all 6 features: var(danceability) + var(energy) + var(valence) + var(acousticness)

Real numbers from the study

Relaxing White Noise
0.009
Meditation Relax Club
0.011
Calmo
0.013
Nils Frahm (organic)
0.183
Key finding: Ghost artist variance is 12–20× lower than organic artists (p < 0.001). Threshold: variance < 0.050 → suspicious.

Data source

Kaggle dataset (114K tracks) — used as a substitute for the Spotify audio-features API (restricted April 2026)

The code

total_variance = df[['danceability','energy','valence','acousticness']].var().sum()
# Ghost threshold: total_variance < 0.050

S2

Release Cadence Synchrony

Are hundreds of songs released on the same day? · Strongest Signal
Think of it like a bookstore vs. a book club.
A regular author publishes one book every year or two — carefully written, edited, promoted. A ghost writing factory dumps 50 books onto Amazon in a single weekend. Ghost artist factories do the same: they batch-upload hundreds of AI-generated tracks all at once, so the release dates cluster tightly.

What it measures

We sort all of an artist's albums/tracks by release date, then measure consecutive gaps (time between releases). The key metric is the closure rate: the percentage of consecutive release pairs where the gap is ≤ 1 day.

A real artist might release an album every 6–18 months. A ghost factory dumps 100 tracks in a weekend — most consecutive pairs have a 0-day gap.

Real numbers from the study

Meditation Relax Club
95%
Relaxing White Noise
81%
Calmo
32%
Nils Frahm (organic)
0%
Key finding: MRC uploads 95% of its catalog in same-day batches. Nils Frahm has zero same-day pairs — every album was released months apart. Threshold: closure rate > 20% → suspicious.

Data source

Spotify API (album release dates) Neo4j (cached)

The code

gaps = np.diff([d.toordinal() for d in sorted_dates])
closure_rate = (gaps == 0).sum() / len(gaps)
# 0.81 for RWN, 0.95 for MRC, 0.000 for Nils Frahm

S3

ISRC Attribution / Playlist Co-occurrence

Who registered this music, and are there network connections? · Context Signal
Think of it like tracking shell companies.
Every published song has an ISRC code — like a serial number. The first 5 characters identify the registrant (record label or production company). If 10 different "artists" all have tracks registered by the same obscure German company, that's suspicious — a real independent artist picks their own registrant.

What it measures

An ISRC code looks like: DE-A4X-21-00001

  • DE = Country (Germany)
  • A4X = Registrant (the production company)
  • 21 = Year 2021
  • 00001 = Serial number

We look at whether multiple "different" artists share the same ISRC registrant — which would mean the same production company is behind them all.

Findings

In our 4-artist study set, no shared registrants were found — each artist used their own companies. This is expected: sophisticated fraud rings use separate registrants per artist to avoid detection. This signal becomes powerful when analyzing hundreds of artists, where shared registrants form visible clusters.

Data source

Neo4j graph database Spotify ISRC metadata

S4

Catalog Density / Upload Velocity

How fast is this artist uploading? · Strong Signal
Think of it like words per minute.
A professional typist does ~80 words per minute. If someone claims to type 2,000 words per minute, you know something's automated. Ghost artist factories produce tracks at inhuman speed — sometimes hundreds per month — because the songs are computer-generated.

What it measures

We compute the artist's upload velocity: total tracks ÷ career span in days = tracks per day. A prolific real artist might do 0.01–0.05 tracks/day. Ghost factories often exceed 0.5–2.0 tracks/day.

S5

Metadata / Title Similarity

Are the track titles suspiciously similar to each other? · Strategy-Specific Signal
Think of it like spam email subject lines.
Spammers generate thousands of emails with titles like "Special Offer for You", "Amazing Deal Today", "Great Savings Now" — they're all semantically the same even if the words vary. Ghost artists that use title templates ("Relaxing Rain Sounds Vol. 1", "Relaxing Rain Sounds Vol. 2", etc.) leave the same fingerprint.

What it measures

We use a sentence-transformers AI model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) to convert every track title into a vector of numbers that captures its meaning. Then we measure how similar all the titles are to each other using cosine similarity (a number from 0 = completely different to 1 = identical meaning).

The mean pairwise cosine across all track title pairs is the signal. Organic artists: ~0.16. Template-based ghosts: ~0.56+.

Real numbers

ArtistMean CosineStrategy
Relaxing White Noise0.557Template titles: "White Noise for Sleeping Vol. X"
Meditation Relax Club0.217Thematic but varied: catches less here
Calmo0.275Mixed strategy
Nils Frahm (organic)~0.16Artistic, varied titles
Nuance: S5 is a strategy-specific detector. It catches ghost artists that use rigid title templates (like RWN). Ghost artists that use more varied naming (like MRC) score lower. This is why you need multiple signals.

The code

from sentence_transformers import SentenceTransformer
model = SentenceTransformer("all-MiniLM-L6-v2")
embs = model.encode(titles, normalize_embeddings=True)
sims = embs @ embs.T  # all pairwise cosines
mean_cos = sims[np.triu_indices(n, k=1)].mean()
# score = clip((mean_cos - 0.10) / 0.50, 0, 1)

S6

Graph Density / HHI Index

Is one production company behind everything? · Most Discriminative Signal
Think of it like market monopoly.
Economists use the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) to measure market concentration. If one company controls 90% of a market, HHI is near 1.0 (monopoly). If 10 companies each have 10%, HHI is 0.10 (competitive). Ghost artists register almost all their tracks through one production company — near-monopoly HHI. Real artists spread across multiple labels over their career.

What it measures

Formula: HHI = Σ(share_i²) where share_i = fraction of tracks registered by company i.

Example: If an artist has 100 tracks, 85 registered by Company A and 15 by Company B:

HHI = (85/100)² + (15/100)² = 0.7225 + 0.0225 = 0.745

Key finding: S6 had the largest discrimination gap of all 7 signals (gap = 0.574). RWN routes 79% of all tracks through a single German registrant. Nils Frahm's tracks come from his own indie label — no concentration at all. Threshold: HHI > 0.40 → suspicious.

S7

Cross-Platform Discrepancy

Does this artist exist outside Spotify? · Supporting Signal
Think of it like a restaurant with no Google Maps listing.
A real restaurant exists on Google Maps, Yelp, TripAdvisor, Instagram, Facebook. A scam "restaurant" that only appears on one food delivery app is suspicious. Similarly, a real musician exists on YouTube, Apple Music, Bandcamp, SoundCloud. A ghost artist factory may only have a Spotify presence.

What it measures

We check two things:

  1. YouTube: Does a search for the artist name return a video? How many views?
  2. Apple Music / iTunes: Does the artist appear in an iTunes search?

Real numbers from the study

ArtistYouTube ViewsApple MusicVerdict
Relaxing White Noise353M viewsYes⚠️ High views but still ghost
Meditation Relax Club157M viewsYes⚠️ Same
Calmo319 viewsNoVery low presence
Nils Frahm9M viewsYesExpected organic range
Important caveat: High YouTube views do NOT mean an artist is legitimate. Relaxing White Noise has 353 million views but is a confirmed fraud. Relaxation and white noise content naturally goes viral on YouTube regardless of origin. S7 is an additional supporting signal, not a disqualifying one.

Data source

YouTube Data API v3 iTunes Search API (free, no auth)

The code
